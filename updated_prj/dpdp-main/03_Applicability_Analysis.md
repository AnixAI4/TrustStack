# Step 2: DPDP Applicability — Who Must Comply?

## The Master Applicability Test

Three questions determine if DPDP applies:
1. Are you processing DIGITAL personal data?
2. Is the data of Indian citizens?
3. Is it for a commercial purpose OR does it cause harm?

If YES to all three → DPDP APPLIES.

---

## What Counts as Personal Data

- Name, Address, Phone, Email
- Aadhaar, PAN, Passport, Voter ID
- Biometrics — fingerprints, face, iris
- Financial data — bank account, credit card, UPI ID
- Health and medical records
- Location data — GPS, IP address
- Caste, religion, political opinions, sexual orientation
- Children's data (extra strict rules, Under 18)
- Device IDs, cookies, behavioral data
- Photos and videos that identify a person
- Employee records — salary, performance, HR data

---

## The 4 Dimensions of Applicability

### Dimension 1: By Geography

| Entity | DPDP Applies? |
|--------|--------------|
| India-based company with Indian users | YES — 100% |
| Foreign company with Indian users | YES — 100% |
| Indian company processing foreign citizens' data (in India) | Likely YES |
| Foreign company with zero Indian users | NO |

### Dimension 2: By Company Size — Data Fiduciary vs Significant Data Fiduciary

**Every company = Data Fiduciary (basic obligations)**
**Some companies = Significant Data Fiduciary (extra obligations)**

SDF criteria: volume of data, sensitivity, national security risk, electoral impact

Expected SDFs: Google, Meta, Amazon, Jio, Airtel, Paytm, PhonePe, Ola, Zomato, Apollo, BYJU's

| Obligation | Regular Company | SDF |
|-----------|----------------|-----|
| Consent Management | Required | Required |
| Data Protection Officer | Not mandatory | MANDATORY |
| Data Protection Impact Assessment | Optional | MANDATORY |
| Annual Audit | Not specified | MANDATORY |
| Algorithmic Transparency | No | Yes |

### Dimension 3: By Industry

**BFSI:** All banks, NBFCs, insurance, MFs, stock brokers, fintech, payment gateways
**Healthcare:** Private hospitals, labs, telemedicine, pharmacy chains, CROs, ABDM entities
**Edtech:** All edtech platforms, K-12 private schools, colleges, coaching institutes
**E-Commerce:** Marketplaces, D2C brands, quick commerce, logistics, social commerce
**Telecom:** Jio, Airtel, Vi + internet platforms
**HR Tech:** Every company with employees (universal applicability)

### Dimension 4: By Data Type

| Tier | Data Type | Rules |
|------|-----------|-------|
| 1 | Standard Personal Data | Standard consent + security |
| 2 | Sensitive Personal Data (health, financial, biometrics) | Explicit consent + DPIAs |
| 3 | Children's Data (Under 18) | Parental consent + no profiling + ₹200 Cr penalty |

---

## What is Exempt

- Offline/paper data never digitized
- Personal/household use
- Publicly available data
- Central government — national security processing
- Research, archiving, statistics (with safeguards)
- Courts and judiciary

---

## Global Applicability

Any company anywhere in the world offering goods/services to Indian citizens must comply.
US, UK, Singapore, Australia-based companies with Indian users are all covered.
**Foreign companies = premium pricing opportunity.**

## Key Business Insight

There is no industry in India's digital economy exempt from DPDP.
Every company with a website, app, CRM, HR system, or customer database is a Data Fiduciary.
The question is not WHETHER they need to comply — it is WHO they will call for help.
