- Beyond enterprise licences (client-hosted or cloud, fee by selected features), add a **Tally-style network**.

### B1.2 Ten lessons

1. **The network is the moat, and daily use holds it together.** Tally partners earn because clients use Tally every day. Our equivalent: Pulse runs, registers, Sentinel alerts, rights and breach clocks, all things a client needs all year.
2. **Partners need recurring income.** A one-off assessment isn't enough. Their clients need monitoring, rights handling, breach support and annual re-checks, which TrustStack already supports.
3. **Price the professional by practice size,** as ClearTax does by GSTINs: packs for 5, 25 or 100 client organisations, with one dashboard over all of them.
4. **Wholesale pricing, not commission.** Zoho's advisors buy at wholesale and set their own fee to their client. For CAs and advocates this is likely the lawful route (B2 confirms against ICAI and Bar Council rules).
5. **Low entry, earned tiers.** Free or low-cost registration and a not-for-resale demo tenant (Vanta); tiers by **work done and quality**, not by fee (Vanta, Tally's stars).
6. **Certification serves adoption, not profit.** OneTrust's is free; DSCI's DCPP costs ~₹15,000 and has standing. Ours should be **reasonably priced, practical (done on the platform), verifiable (badge + public directory) and renewed yearly**.
7. **A separate academy earns and feeds the network,** like Tally Education: courses for professionals, students and client staff (F-21, F-65).
8. **Certification isn't quality:** Tally says so itself. We need a code of conduct, sample reviews of partner work, client feedback and removal for misconduct.
9. **A public directory of certified professionals** helps clients find partners and partners find clients. It must respect each profession's advertising rules (B2).
10. **Complement DSCI, don't fight it.** A partner can hold DSCI DCPP (the law and privacy knowledge) **and** TrustStack certification (doing DPDP work on our platform).

## Step B2 — Professional rules: what CAs, company secretaries and advocates may lawfully do

**Read on 29 Oct 2026:**
- **CA Act 1949**, First Schedule Part I: clauses (2), (3), (6), (7) and (11), as reproduced by tilakmarg.com and barelaws.com (the official India Code PDF refused automated reads).
- **ICAI material:** commentary on the Code of Ethics; the "management consultancy and other services" permission; disciplinary cases.
- **Bar Council of India Rules**, Part VI Chapter II: **Rule 36** (advertising, with the 2008 website proviso and the **2025 BCI enforcement directives**) and **Rules 47–52** (other business and employment).
- **Company Secretaries Act 1980**, First Schedule Part I (MCA text, ICSI journal commentary).

> **Not legal advice.** This is a product and business reading of the rules, so we design within them.
> **Before launch, get a written opinion from counsel**, and ideally an ICAI Expert Advisory Committee
> opinion and ICSI guidance, on the final partner terms. (Q4)

### B2.1 What each profession's rules say (in plain words)

| Rule | **Chartered accountants in practice** (CA Act, 1st Sch. Part I) | **Company secretaries in practice** (CS Act, 1st Sch. Part I) | **Advocates** (BCI Rules, Part VI Ch. II) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Paying a share or commission of their fees to a non-member** | **Misconduct** (clause 2): "pays or allows … any share, commission or brokerage in the fees or profits" to anyone outside members, partners and listed professionals | **Misconduct** (clause 2), the same structure | No explicit rule found; Rule 36 bars **touts** and indirect solicitation, so paying anyone for work is unsafe |
| **Accepting a share of a non-member's profits** (e.g. a vendor's commission) | **Misconduct** (clause 3): "accepts … any part of the profits of the professional work of a person who is not a member", except with members or listed professionals. **Commentary based on IFAC treats vendor commissions as a self-interest threat** | Similar | Running a business is barred (Rule 47) |
| **Soliciting or advertising** | **Misconduct** (clauses 6, 7): no soliciting by circular, advertisement or personal communication; no advertising of attainments; only a factual **write-up** under ICAI guidelines; **no non-approved designations** on professional documents | Similar, with a guideline-based write-up allowed | **Rule 36:** "shall not solicit work or advertise, either directly or indirectly"; a website only in the **BCI-approved form** (2008); **2025:** BCI directed the removal of violating advertisements, including on platforms |
| **Other business** (e.g. reselling software for a margin) | **Misconduct** (clause 11) **unless the Council permits it**. Members with other-business permission **may not do attest work**. Training and certain consultancy are allowed as "management consultancy and other services" | **Misconduct** (clause 10) unless permitted | **Rule 47:** "shall not personally engage in any business"; may be a **sleeping partner** only if the State Bar Council finds it consistent with the profession's dignity. Rule 48: a non-executive director only. **The Supreme Court upheld a 5-year suspension of an advocate working as a real-estate agent** |
| **Using a software tool in their practice** | Allowed: buying tools is part of running a practice, not a fee share or a business | Allowed | Allowed |
| **Being trained and certified by a vendor** | Allowed, but **the designation can't go on professional documents** (clause 7) | Similar | Allowed, but **can't be advertised** (Rule 36) |

**Other regulated professions** (cost accountants under the CMA Act 1959, and
others) have similar codes; treat them as "regulated professionals" until
checked. **Unregulated firms** (IT services, cyber-security, managed service
providers, management consultancies whose staff aren't practising CAs, CSs or
advocates) are bound only by contract law, GST and the Consumer Protection
Act. **They may resell and earn commissions.**

### B2.2 What this means for the TrustStack partner design

| Idea from B1 | Safe for regulated professionals? | Design rule |
|---|---|---|
| Commission or referral fee **paid to** a CA / CS / advocate for sending clients to TrustStack | ❌ Clause 3 (CA/CS); business and touting (advocates) | **No commissions to regulated professionals.** Ever |
| A CA / CS / advocate **paying TrustStack per lead** or for a tier that brings work | ❌ Clause 2 (paying a non-member for work); Rule 36 (touts) | **No pay-per-lead and no paid tiers that buy work.** Tiers are earned by quality, and leads are free and chosen by the client |
| A CA **reselling TrustStack licences for a margin** | ⚠️ "Other business": needs ICAI Council permission, and then **no attest work**, which most CAs won't accept | **Regulated professionals don't resell.** The client buys from TrustStack, or the professional buys a tool for their own practice |
| A professional **buying a practice subscription** and billing clients their own professional fee | ✅ Buying a tool for one's own practice | **Route A: "Practice" licence** |
| The client **buys TrustStack directly** and invites its CA or advocate as a consultant user | ✅ No money between TrustStack and the professional | **Route B: "Invite your professional"** |
| **Paid training and certification** for professionals | ✅ | **Route C: Academy + certification**, open to all |
| A "TrustStack Certified" **designation on letterheads or reports** | ❌ Clause 7 (CA); Rule 36 (advocates) | **Badge use rules:** a verifiable personal credential; **not on professional stationery or statutory documents** for regulated professionals |
| A **public directory** of certified professionals | ⚠️ Indirect solicitation risk (CA clause 6; Rule 36, and BCI's 2025 directions to platforms) | **A factual, opt-in, unranked listing** (name, city, qualification, contact only; no claims, no reviews, no "top partner"). **Advocates: excluded** unless counsel confirms a BCI-compliant format |
| Sales and referral commissions for **unregulated firms** (IT, cyber, MSPs, consultancies) | ✅ Contract + GST | **Route D: Business partners** may resell and earn a margin or a referral fee (as NewCo's plan already allows for cyber firms and MSPs) |
| The word **"agent"** | ⚠️ Implies a business agency, which regulated professionals can't run | Use **"TrustStack Certified DPDP Professional"** for individuals; **"Authorised Partner"** only for unregulated firms |

### B2.3 The four lawful routes to market

```
                          ┌──────────────── money ────────────────┐
 A. PRACTICE LICENCE      Professional ──pays──► TrustStack          Professional ──bills own fee──► Client
    (CA, CS, CMA, advocate,  (tool for own practice; Pulse included; per-client modules)
     consultant)
 B. INVITE YOUR           Client ──pays──► TrustStack                Client ──pays own fee──► Professional
    PROFESSIONAL             (client's own licence; professional is a consultant user; no money between them)
 C. ACADEMY +             Anyone ──pays──► TrustStack Academy        (course + exam + kit + badge; yearly renewal)
    CERTIFICATION
 D. AUTHORISED PARTNER    Client ──pays──► Partner ──wholesale──► TrustStack   (or TrustStack pays a referral fee)
    (UNREGULATED firms only: IT services, cyber-security, MSPs, consultancies without practising CAs/CSs/advocates)
```

**Every route keeps the DPDP roles right.** The client is the **Data
Fiduciary**. TrustStack, and the professional where they handle the client's
data on the platform, act **on its behalf** (s.8(1), s.8(2)). The contracts
form a chain (client ↔ TrustStack DPA; professional's engagement letter), and
the client must be able to remove a professional's access at any time.

### B2.4 Fit with the NewCo plan

The NewCo plan (`company_formation/02_Business_Model.md` §2.5) already says
**law and CA firms get no referral fees (Bar Council / ICAI rules)**, while
cyber firms and MSPs may earn 10–15 % or a reseller margin. **B2 confirms that
line** and adds Routes A–C, so CAs and advocates can still be a large
network without any fee-sharing. One point to check against the plan: **one of
the Group of 5 is a practising CA**. The commission and equity in the
formation pack must be cleared against ICAI clauses (2), (3) and (11) (a
practising CA as a shareholder or salesperson of a company). That belongs in
the formation pack and with counsel, not here.

### B4.2 Professional track — certification

**Two certification levels, matching the assessment levels** (Pulse → Proof),
so what a professional is certified for is exactly what they do on the platform.

| | **Level 1 — TrustStack Certified DPDP Professional (Pulse Practitioner)** | **Level 2 — TrustStack Certified DPDP Assessor (Proof)** |
|---|---|---|
| **Can do** | Run Pulse for clients, explain results, build the action plan, set up Essentials (notice, consent, rights, breach basics), Sentinel Essentials | Run Proof: collect and **review evidence** (as a reviewer who is not the uploader, D-043), registers in depth, Board reports, Sentinel tiers, reassessments |
| **Who** | Any professional or consultant | Level 1 holders with practice experience (e.g. 3 Pulse clients done) |
| **Learning** | ~12–15 hours online: the Act and Rules in plain words (with Gazette citations), the 16 domains, Pulse on the platform, talking to clients, ethics for professionals (B2) | ~20–25 hours: evidence and the cap, ROPA in depth, breach (Rule 7) and rights (Rule 14) handling, retention, processors, SDF readiness, exposure and Board reporting |
| **Exam** | MCQ (law + platform) **+ a practical**: run Pulse on a fictional client in the demo organisation and produce the action plan; pass mark 70 % | MCQ **+ a practical**: review an evidence set, reach the right capped scores, write the Board summary; marked by AnixAI |
| **Credential** | A verifiable digital badge + certificate ID; an optional factual directory listing (B2 rules) | The same |
| **Validity** | **1 year**; renewal = a short law-update module (Rules changes, Board orders, Sentinel's law watch) + an active practice or a refresher exam | 1 year, the same + a quality score above the threshold |

**Positioning against DSCI DCPP** (~₹15,000, 3 years, B1): **complementary.**
DCPP proves privacy knowledge; TrustStack certification proves the person can
**deliver DPDP work on our platform**. The Academy can offer DCPP-prep content
later.

**Badge rules (B2):** a personal credential. It isn't printed on professional
stationery, audit reports or statutory documents by practising CAs, CSs or
advocates, and it never means "government-certified" or that a client is
"compliant".

### B4.6 Quality — protecting the brand (Tally's lesson: certification ≠ quality)

1. **A code of conduct**, signed at registration: honesty with clients, no "compliant" or "certified" claims about a client (D-011), confidentiality, DPDP-safe handling of client data, the professional's own rules come first (B2), no misuse of the badge.
2. **A quality score per partner, from the platform's own data:** Pulse completion, how complete the registers are, evidence review accuracy (Level 2), client feedback after each Pulse and Proof, complaints.
3. **Sample reviews:** AnixAI reviews a sample of each Assessor's Proof work every year (e.g. 1 in 10, at least one).
4. **Client feedback:** a short rating after each Pulse and Proof, sent to the client, never visible as a public star rating (B2).
5. **A sanctions ladder:** a note → a remediation plan → suspension → removal (badge revoked, directory listing removed, licence not renewed). The client keeps its data and can move to another professional.
6. **A complaints channel** for clients, in the platform and published.

### B4.7 Leads and the directory (B2-safe)

- **The directory is chosen by the client:** a factual list (name, city, qualifications, languages, industries served), **opt-in, unranked, alphabetical or by distance**. Advocates are listed only after counsel clears a format.
- **No lead selling, no pay-for-position, no "top partner" badges** for professionals.
- **Enterprise leads** that suit an authorised partner (e.g. a client-hosted install) are routed by AnixAI under the Route D agreement.
- **A client may invite any professional** to its own TrustStack (Route B), even one who isn't certified. The professional then works as a consultant user with limited rights.

### B4.8 Data protection inside the programme

| Situation | Roles and safeguards |
|---|---|
| A professional uses a practice licence for a client | The **client** is the Data Fiduciary; the professional and TrustStack act on its behalf. The professional's **engagement letter** has a data-processing clause; TrustStack's terms cover the professional (s.8(2)) |
| The client invites a professional (Route B) | The client's own licence and DPA with TrustStack; the professional is a **consultant user** the client can remove at any time; every access audited |
| A professional leaves the programme | The client's data stays with the client; it can export everything or move to another professional; the professional's access ends |
| Certification and the directory | AnixAI is the Data Fiduciary for **partners' own personal data** (profile, exam, badge): a notice, consent for the directory listing, the right to withdraw it |

### B4.9 The journeys

**A CA becomes a certified professional (target: under 2 weeks):**
1. Registers (free) → Member; opens the demo organisation.
2. Buys the certification package (course + exam + starter kit + Practice Starter year 1).
3. Learns (~12–15 h) → takes the MCQ + practical Pulse → **Certified**; badge issued.
4. Runs Pulse with 2–3 existing clients in the first month; shares the client-education pack with existing clients only.
5. Upgrades a serious client to Proof (Level 2 later), or the client buys Professional directly and invites the CA (Route B).
6. Renews yearly with the law-update module.

**An IT services firm becomes an authorised partner:**
1. Signs the agreement → Registered; one person certified.
2. Registers its first deals → referral fees.
3. Certifies more staff and a deployment engineer → Silver / Gold; resells and installs client-hosted editions.

## Step B6 — Revenue model and unit economics

**Built on:** B3 (editions, practice packs, streams, waves), B4 (certification and kit), B1 and the consolidated Step 4 (market prices), and **the NewCo plan's own prices and per-deal economics** for enterprise and consulting (`company_formation/02_*`, `08_*`, kept as they are). **Confidential pieces** (authorised-partner margins; how the NewCo ↔ LLP split applies to the new streams) are in `company_formation/14_Partner_Economics_Confidential.md`, **outside this repo**.

All prices: ₹, ex-GST, per year unless marked. **Proposals for approval, not yet a published price list.**

### B6.1 Pricing principles

1. **Published prices** for small businesses, professionals and learners (simple and trusted, as Tally and Zoho do); **quoted prices** for enterprise.
2. **The same price for every professional** of the same pack size. No discount bought by volume of leads, and no paid placement (B2).
3. **Priced by what the customer recognises:** organisations by edition and size; professionals by number of client organisations; learners per course.
4. **Recurring first:** annual plans; certification renews yearly.
5. **Below OneTrust, above the cheap tools, justified by proof:** Pulse, Proof, ₹ exposure, Sentinel.

## Step B7 — Platform requirements: one flexible platform for every model

**Checked in the code (29 Oct 2026, `main`):**
- **Tenancy is membership-based.** RLS lets a user see only organisations they belong to (`app.current_user_organization_ids()`, D-019).
- **The `consultant` role** ("runs assessments for the client organisations they are assigned to") already gives a person access to several client organisations through membership, with an organisation switcher.
- **Invitations** exist (Slice 1); **there is no self sign-up**.
- Organisations have `organization_type`, `consultant_assisted` and `parent_organization_id`.
- Feature selection, licensing and billing aren't built (Slice 14, F-67, F-68).

### B7.1 The one design choice that keeps it safe

**A partner reaches a client's data only by being a member of that client's
organisation**, exactly as an AnixAI consultant does today. There's no
cross-tenant shortcut and no new RLS path. A new **partner engagement**
record says who brought whom, who pays, and when the client agreed. **The
client always sees, and can remove, every outside person with access.** That
keeps Gate 5 (Org A can never reach Org B) intact, and the DPDP chain
(client = Data Fiduciary; partner and TrustStack act on its behalf) visible
in the product.
