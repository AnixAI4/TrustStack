## DPDP in plain words

**The law in five sentences.**

1.  If you hold a person's data in digital form, you must tell them clearly what you collect and why, in English or any of the 22 scheduled languages (s.5, Rule 3).

2.  You may use it only with their consent or for a few legitimate uses the Act lists, such as a duty under another law like KYC (s.6, s.7).

3.  You must keep it safe, report a breach to the Data Protection Board and to every affected person, and send the Board a full report within 72 hours (s.8(5)–(6), Rule 7).

4.  You must delete it when the purpose is over, unless another law says keep it (s.8(7)).

5.  People can ask to see, correct or erase their data and complain, and you must answer within 90 days (ss.11–14, Rule 14).

**Terms you will hear.**

| Term | Plain meaning | Bank example |
|----|----|----|
| Data Principal | The person the data is about | The customer, borrower, guarantor, employee |
| Data Fiduciary | Whoever decides why and how data is used, and answers for it | The bank or NBFC |
| Data Processor | Whoever handles data on the fiduciary's behalf | Cloud provider, call centre, collection agency, KYC vendor |
| Significant Data Fiduciary (SDF) | A big or sensitive fiduciary the government names for extra duties | Likely the large banks and big NBFCs (none named yet) |
| Consent Manager | A registered platform that lets people give and withdraw consent across firms | Similar idea to an Account Aggregator; registration opens 13 Nov 2026 |
| Data Protection Board | The regulator that hears complaints and imposes penalties | Up to ₹250 crore for weak security |
| Legitimate use | A reason to use data without consent | KYC and anti-money-laundering records |
| ROPA | Record of processing activities: the bank's list of what data it uses, why, where and with whom | "Home loan onboarding uses PAN, income and bureau report; shared with the credit bureau; kept 5+ years" |

**One sentence on penalties:** the Board can fine up to ₹250 crore for failing to keep data safe, ₹200 crore for not reporting a breach, and ₹200 crore for children's data failures (Schedule to the Act).

What DPDP means for each family, in one line:

- **Commercial banks:** the biggest customer bases and the most likely SDFs. They buy through tenders and want proof, scale and on-premises options.

- **Cooperative banks:** thin IT and compliance teams on shared vendors. They are the least prepared, and their tier decides how much RBI expects.

- **NBFCs:** digital lending, agents and collection firms make them data-hungry and complaint-prone. The 656 Middle Layer NBFCs are our sweet spot; the 8,711 in the Base Layer need a low-cost kit.

**How to say it:** "Nine in ten of the 11,400 lenders RBI regulates are small NBFCs and cooperative banks. They have the same law and far less help."

## Why banks must act now

Five reasons, in the order a banker feels them:

1.  **The clock is short.** RBI's explicit-consent rules for selling products apply on **1 Jan 2027**: every product needs its own recorded "yes", and screens must default to "No". DPDP's main duties apply on **13 May 2027**. A bank on legacy systems needs most of that time just to build.

2.  **RBI is already checking.** Advisory 3/2026 (March 2026) sets out what good customer-data protection looks like. The July 2026 data governance draft and cyber rules (6-hour incident reports) follow the same line. Expect supervisors to ask about it in inspections, likely before the Data Protection Board even sits.

3.  **The fines are large, and boards will be asked about them.** Up to ₹250 crore for weak security and ₹200 crore for not reporting a breach. Under RBI's new consent rules, mis-selling means a full refund plus compensation.

4.  **Breaches are now public.** SBI told the stock exchanges on 21 Oct 2026 that 3,897 customer records were found on the dark web. Bank of Baroda had an incident in July. After 13 May 2027 each such event needs a 72-hour report to the Board and a notice to every affected customer.

5.  **Peers are moving.** Indian Bank, Bank of Baroda, PNB, India Post Payments Bank and Dhanlaxmi Bank all issued DPDP tenders in 2026. A board that has not started will be asked why.

**How to say it:** "This is not a 2027 problem. Consent rules bite in January, RBI is inspecting now, and one leak puts the bank in the news."

### TrustStack, in plain words

TrustStack is the bank's DPDP control room. It asks the right questions for a bank (our banking question pack) and accepts a claim only when there is evidence behind it. From that it builds the score, the gap list, the registers and the Board report, and keeps them current.

What it does today:

- **Assessment and score:** 16 DPDP areas; answers are capped by the evidence behind them, so the score cannot be inflated.

- **Registers:** ROPA (what data, why, where, with whom), gap and action tracker with owners and deadlines.

- **Workflows:** breach handling (with the 72-hour clock), rights requests and grievances, notices and consent, vendors, retention and erasure, AI-use risk.

- **Board view:** dashboards, penalty-exposure estimate, Board brief, DPO report, regulator-ready evidence pack.

- **AI assistant:** drafts notices, breach intimations and replies for a person to check, using the Act and Rules as its source.

What it does **not** do yet (say this honestly if asked):

- It does not scan the bank's systems to find personal data automatically; we map data through workshops and imports.

- It does not capture consent at scale in 22 languages inside the bank's apps; it works alongside the bank's consent tool.

- It is not yet packaged to install inside the bank's own data centre. That is planned and can be prioritised for the pilot bank.

- It is hosted in India (Mumbai region), but formal certifications such as ISO 27001 are still to come.

## Likely questions, and what to say

Short answers first; the legal reference is there if he pushes. Where a point is still open, say so. That builds more trust than a confident guess.

### About DPDP and banks

**1. We already follow RBI's KYC and cyber rules. Isn't that enough?** It covers much of the security side, but DPDP adds customer-facing duties RBI never fully covered: a clear notice per purpose, consent that can be withdrawn as easily as given, answers to access and correction requests within 90 days, erasure when the purpose ends, and a 72-hour report to the Data Protection Board. RBI's own Advisory 3/2026 points the same way.

**2. Will DPDP force us to delete KYC records?** No. Data that a law requires you to keep stays (s.8(7)); the Act's own example is a bank keeping a closed account's records. What must go is data no law protects, such as rejected applications, old marketing lists and copies kept "just in case".

**3. Can we still pursue defaulters?** Yes. The Act exempts processing to trace a defaulter's assets (s.17(1)(f)) and to enforce legal claims (s.17(1)(a)). Security duties still apply, and collection agents' handling of data is still the bank's responsibility.

**4. Are we a Significant Data Fiduciary?** No bank has been named yet. Large banks and big NBFCs are the likely candidates, given their volume of financial data. Being ready for a yearly DPIA and audit is sensible either way.

**5. We report cyber incidents to RBI within 6 hours. Does DPDP add anything?** Yes, two things. The Data Protection Board and every affected customer must be told, with a detailed report to the Board within 72 hours (Rule 7). And a "personal data breach" is wider than a cyber incident: an email sent to the wrong people counts. One process should run all three clocks.

**6. What about Account Aggregators and the new Consent Managers?** AAs carry on under RBI. Whether an AA must also register as a DPDP Consent Manager from 13 Nov 2026 is still open. As a provider and user of AA data, the bank should keep one clean record of all consents.

**7. Will the deadline move?** MeitY floated cutting the 18 months to 12 for large players in January 2026, but nothing has been notified. Plan for 13 May 2027, and for 1 Jan 2027 on RBI's consent rules.

### About us and TrustStack

**8. How are you different from a Big Four firm?** They deliver a report and often resell a foreign tool. We deliver the fixes and leave a working platform the bank keeps using, at a price a mid-sized lender can approve.

**9. How are you different from consent-management tools?** Those capture consent in apps, and do it well. TrustStack is the layer above: assessment, evidence, registers, breach and rights workflows, and Board reporting across DPDP and RBI. We work alongside the bank's consent tool, not against it.

**10. Do you have banking clients?** Be straight: "Not yet in banking. The platform is built and running with a university, and our BFSI content is ready. That is why we want one pilot institution: to prove it in banking under your eye." *(Add your own banking experience here.)*

**11. Where is the data hosted? Can it run inside our bank?** On servers in India (Mumbai region). An edition that installs inside the bank's own data centre or cloud is planned, and we can prioritise it for the pilot institution.

**12. Is your AI safe with customer data?** The AI assistant only drafts (notices, breach letters, replies), and a person approves every draft. It can be switched off. During a pilot it needs no customer records, only policy-level information.

**13. What data would you see during a pilot?** Mostly process information: policies, system lists, vendor lists, sample notices. Not customer records. We sign an NDA and a data processing agreement before we start.

**14. Can you serve small cooperative banks and NBFCs cheaply?** Yes. A guided, mostly self-serve package for small lenders, reached through their associations, is part of our plan.

### About the pilot and money

**15. What would a pilot look like?** Eight to ten weeks with one institution. Outputs: a baseline score, a ranked gap list, a processing register for three to five key customer journeys, a breach playbook, a consent review against the 1 Jan 2027 rules, and a Board pack.

**16. What does it cost?** Give a range, not a number, and say pricing is confirmed after a short scoping call. Working range for a mid-sized NBFC: ₹10–25 lakh for assessment and fixes, and ₹5–15 lakh a year for the platform. Step 3 will confirm this.

**17. What do you need from us?** An executive sponsor, a compliance or DPO lead, three or four process owners (lending, operations, IT, customer service), our document request list, and two hours a week of their time.

### About RBI

**18. What would you want RBI to see?** A short, neutral note on sector readiness and a tested way to get there, not a sales pitch. The pilot gives evidence: what a lender's gaps really look like, and a control map linking DPDP to RBI's directions that RBI, IBA or the NBFC self-regulatory body could adopt as a common baseline.

**19. Why would RBI care?** Customer trust and consistency. Around 10,000 of the 11,400 lenders are small and have little in-house expertise. A common, affordable baseline lifts the whole sector before the Data Protection Board starts fining.

**20. Are you asking RBI to endorse you?** No. We are offering evidence and a reusable framework. Any endorsement is RBI's decision alone.

## Questions to ask him

Ask these to shape the pilot and the RBI note. His answers decide Steps 3 to 6.

**About his institution and the pilot**

- [ ] Which institution would you suggest for the pilot: an NBFC (which layer?), a small finance bank or a cooperative bank?

- [ ] Roughly how many customers, branches and lending apps does it have?

- [ ] Has it done a DPDP gap assessment yet? Who owns DPDP there: compliance, CISO, legal or a DPO?

- [ ] Which core banking or lending system and which consent tool does it use, and does it allow cloud SaaS?

- [ ] What would success look like for you after 8 to 10 weeks?

**About the RBI note**

- [ ] Who at RBI would read it, and through which channel: directly, IBA, the NBFC self-regulatory body, or the Department of Financial Services?

- [ ] What format works best: a two-page note, a short deck, or both?

- [ ] Should it go before the pilot (as a proposal) or after (with findings)?

**About working together**

- [ ] How would you like to be involved: advisor, introducer or board member? Should we formalise that?

- [ ] Can we sign a mutual NDA before we share the demo and pricing?

## What not to say

He may forward what you say to the regulator, so every claim must survive a careful reader.

| Avoid saying | Say instead | Why |
|----|----|----|
| "RBI-approved" or "DPDP-certified" | "Aligned to the Act, the Rules and RBI's directions" | No certification exists under the Act; RBI has endorsed nothing |
| "100% compliant" or "no penalties" | "Ready, with the evidence to show it" | No one can promise outcomes the Board decides |
| "TrustStack finds all your personal data" | "We map your data with your teams, and TrustStack keeps the map current" | No automatic discovery yet |
| "Runs in your data centre today" | "An in-premises edition is planned; we can prioritise it for you" | Not built yet |
| "You are an SDF" | "You are a likely candidate; prepare as if you were" | None notified yet |
| "The deadline moved to November 2026" | "13 May 2027; a shorter timeline was proposed but not notified" | MeitY proposal only |
| Quoting RBI Advisory 3/2026 word for word | "RBI's March 2026 advisory on customer data protection" | It is not on RBI's public site; our copy came from a law firm |
| Named criticism of competitors | "Most offer either consulting or a consent tool" | Sounds petty; may reach people who know them |
| Showing demo data as real bank data | "A fictional bank with synthetic data" | Real-looking data under a real name could mislead |
| A firm legal answer on open points (AA vs Consent Manager, exact retention periods) | "Counsel is confirming this; here is the current view" | These are genuinely unsettled |
| Details of the NewCo terms | "We are forming a dedicated company with experienced partners" | Confidential |
