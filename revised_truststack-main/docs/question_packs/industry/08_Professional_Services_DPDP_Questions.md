## 1. How to use this industry file

This file is an **industry overlay**. It should not replace the core DPDP Compass assessment.

The correct assessment design is:

```text
Core DPDP Compass 16-domain assessment
+ Professional Services industry overlay
+ Conditional packs: legal/professional privilege, audit/tax working papers, recruitment/candidate data, data rooms, freelancers/subcontractors, cloud collaboration, AI use, cross-border access, breach
+ Organisation-specific dynamic questions for practice areas, engagements, matters, systems, vendors, portals, shared drives and ROPA records
```

The Professional Services overlay should be used for:

- management consulting, IT consulting, risk consulting and advisory firms;
- law firms, legal process outsourcing and litigation-support teams;
- chartered accountancy, audit, assurance and tax firms;
- company secretary, governance, compliance and secretarial advisory firms;
- cost accounting, valuation, insolvency, transaction advisory and due-diligence practices;
- recruitment, staffing, executive search and HR advisory firms;
- marketing, PR, creative, research and analytics agencies;
- architecture, engineering, project-management and design consultancies;
- shared-service centres, offshore delivery centres and professional-services networks;
- small professional practices that use email, WhatsApp, cloud drives and SaaS tools heavily.;

Important positioning:

> Professional-services firms are high-trust, document-heavy and people-intensive organisations. They may hold extremely sensitive client files but may not see themselves as “data businesses”. DPDP Compass must therefore assess client confidentiality, engagement-level data use, unstructured documents, external experts, AI tools, retention, breach readiness and role clarity with unusual depth.

### 2.4 Professional-services-specific DPDP distinction

| Processing context | DPDP Compass implication |
|---|---|
| Direct client onboarding by the firm | Firm likely decides what client-contact, billing, KYC, conflict-check and engagement data to collect. |
| Professional engagement for a client | Role depends on engagement terms and who determines purpose/means; legal review required for ambiguous cases. |
| Outsourced back-office/service processing for a client | Firm may be closer to Data Processor for client-controlled processing; contract and instructions become critical. |
| Audit/tax/legal/secretarial working papers | Retention, confidentiality, professional obligations and rights/erasure handling require careful review. |
| Candidate or recruitment processing | Firm may collect personal data directly from candidates and disclose profiles to clients. |
| Due diligence / data rooms | Large-volume, time-bound, multi-party access, high sensitivity and cross-border review risks. |
| AI-assisted drafting/research/review | Prompts, documents, embeddings, logs and vendor training/retention terms must be controlled. |
| Marketing and thought leadership | Use of client contacts, event lists, case studies, testimonials and newsletters needs purpose/consent review. |
| Global networks / offshore delivery | Cross-border access, inter-firm arrangements, cloud regions and client approval must be checked. |
| Freelancers and external experts | Human subcontractors are Data Processor/vendor-like risks and must be governed contractually. |

## 8. Key DPDP risks in Professional Services

| Risk | Explanation |
|---|---|
| Role ambiguity | The firm may be Data Fiduciary for some processing and Data Processor-like for client-instructed processing. |
| Unstructured data sprawl | Most personal data sits in emails, attachments, drives, PDFs, spreadsheets, scans and chat messages. |
| Client confidentiality overlap | Professional confidentiality, legal privilege, audit records and client instructions complicate rights/erasure handling. |
| Excessive client data collection | Teams may request complete data dumps instead of minimum necessary data. |
| Third-party human access | Freelancers, consultants, retired experts, interns and offshore teams may access files. |
| AI leakage risk | Staff may upload client data into public or unapproved AI tools. |
| Data-room and due-diligence risk | Bulk datasets with many Data Principals can be shared with many parties. |
| Email and wrong-recipient risk | Misdirected emails and wrong attachments are common breach scenarios. |
| Retention uncertainty | Old client files, working papers, email archives and lost proposals may be retained indefinitely. |
| Cross-border ambiguity | Cloud tools, global member firms and offshore delivery may create unknown data-location or access risk. |
| Marketing misuse | Client contacts, event attendees and case studies may be reused without clear preference management. |
| Small-firm informality | Smaller practices may rely on WhatsApp, personal devices and local folders without governance. |

### 9.1 GOV — Governance, accountability and DPDP ownership

| Code | Assessment question | Why it matters | Evidence to check | Suggested owner | Priority | Type |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## 13. Red flags and scoring considerations

| Red flag | Severity | Suggested score-cap / action logic |
|---|---|---|
| No DPDP/privacy owner for firm data and client files | Critical | Cap overall score at 60; create P0 governance action |
| No engagement-level role classification | Critical | Cap at 55 for professional-services firms handling client personal data |
| No inventory of email/shared drives/DMS/client repositories | Critical | Cap at 55; create data discovery sprint |
| No ROPA for major service lines | Critical | Cap at 60; create ROPA completion action |
| No client-data handling policy covering confidentiality + DPDP + AI | Critical | Cap at 60; create policy/training action |
| Use of unapproved public AI tools with client documents | Critical | Cap at 50; immediate AI containment action |
| Freelancers/subcontractors access client files without NDA/DPA/access control | High/Critical | Cap at 65 or 55 depending volume/sensitivity |
| No breach process for misdirected email or wrong data-room access | High | Create P0/P1 breach readiness action |
| No client notification route for breach involving client-controlled data | Critical | Cap at 60; create engagement-letter and breach workflow action |
| No retention schedule for working papers, client files and email archives | High | Cap at 70; create retention governance action |
