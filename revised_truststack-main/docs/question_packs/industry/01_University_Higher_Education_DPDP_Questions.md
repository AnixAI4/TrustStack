## 2. DPDP context for universities and higher education institutions

Universities and higher education institutions process large amounts of personal data across the full applicant, student, alumni, faculty, employee, research and campus lifecycle. They often collect data before admission, maintain academic records for long periods, publish award data, use ERP/LMS/exam systems, share data with regulators and platforms, engage many SaaS vendors and increasingly deploy AI-enabled tools.

Typical university data processing includes admissions, entrance tests, student registration, examinations, marksheets, transcripts, LMS usage, hostel and transport records, scholarship and category documents, placements, alumni engagement, research participant data, grievance records, HR records, CCTV/access logs, AI chatbots, AI proctoring, analytics and automation.

The assessment should treat universities as **multi-process, multi-system, multi-stakeholder data ecosystems**, not as one administrative unit.

## 7. Key DPDP risks in universities

| Risk area | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Large student data lifecycle | Data is collected before admission and retained long after graduation |
| Under-18 students | Some applicants and first-year students may be below 18 |
| Multiple collection points | Website, forms, portals, paper, events, WhatsApp, email |
| Weak notice linkage | Privacy notices may not be shown at every data collection point |
| Unclear consent records | Consent may be bundled with admission terms or not logged |
| Fragmented systems | ERP, LMS, exams, HRMS, payment, hostel, placement and alumni systems may not be integrated |
| Vendor dependency | Many SaaS vendors process student/faculty data |
| Affiliated-college data flows | Universities may receive/share records with colleges |
| Examination confidentiality | Exam/result data needs strong access and retention controls |
| Academic records accuracy | Marksheets, transcripts and award records require high accuracy |
| NAD/DigiLocker publication | Academic award data may be shared/uploaded through official repositories |
| Grievance and disciplinary records | Sensitive records need restricted access and retention rules |
| Research data | Surveys/interviews may include personal data and special contextual sensitivity |
| AI tools | Chatbots, proctoring, analytics and GenAI tools can create new privacy risks |
| Evidence weakness | Policies may exist, but implementation proof may be missing |
