## 0. Important positioning

This file is an **industry overlay**, not a replacement for the core DPDP Compass question bank.

Every EdTech organisation should first complete the **core 16-domain DPDP Compass assessment**. The questions in this file are additional, sector-specific questions that help assess EdTech-specific processing risks such as student onboarding, parent/guardian data, children’s data, learning analytics, AI tutors, proctoring, app tracking, marketing funnels, subscriptions, payment data, LMS integrations, school/college partnerships, video classes, counsellor access, teacher/tutor access, and cloud/vendor processing.

This assessment output should be used as **DPDP readiness and implementation guidance**, not as a final legal opinion, statutory certification, or guarantee of compliance. Legal interpretation, policy approval, contract sign-off, children’s data decisions, cross-border decisions, and consent model decisions should be reviewed by authorised legal counsel.

## 1. EdTech DPDP context

EdTech organisations are high-priority candidates for DPDP assessment because they often combine:

- direct-to-student digital onboarding,
- minors/children’s data,
- parent/guardian data,
- learning behaviour and performance analytics,
- video/live-class platforms,
- AI tutors and recommendation engines,
- mobile apps and tracking SDKs,
- marketing funnels and counsellor calls,
- subscription and payment journeys,
- school, college, university or coaching-centre partnerships,
- third-party LMS, cloud, CRM, payment, analytics and communication vendors.

The EdTech assessment must therefore examine the full lifecycle of personal data: collection, recording, organisation, structuring, storage, adaptation, retrieval, use, disclosure, sharing, dissemination, erasure and destruction. In the DPDP Compass model, EdTech is not treated as only an education business; it is treated as a **digital platform, learning platform, consumer platform, child-data platform, analytics platform, AI platform and vendor-driven SaaS ecosystem**.

## 7. Key EdTech DPDP risk themes

| Risk theme | Why it matters in EdTech |
|---|---|
| Children’s data | K-12 platforms may process under-18 learner data and parent/guardian data |
| Over-collection | Free content, diagnostic tests, webinars and downloads may collect more data than needed |
| Consent bundling | Service, marketing, profiling, AI and third-party sharing may be mixed together |
| Weak withdrawal | Users may not have easy unsubscribe/withdrawal or account deletion options |
| Learning analytics profiling | Behavioural data can create sensitive inferences about ability, attention, weakness or career pathway |
| AI tutors/recommendations | Personal data may enter prompts, embeddings, models or automated profiling workflows |
| Video/live-class data | Recordings, chat, attendance and screen data can be sensitive, especially for minors |
| Proctoring and monitoring | Webcam, audio, screen and device monitoring are high-risk and need strong justification/review |
| Counsellor/CRM access | Large sales teams may access learner and parent data without enough need-to-know controls |
| Vendor sprawl | EdTech platforms often use many SaaS vendors, SDKs, APIs and marketing tools |
| Cross-border uncertainty | Global SaaS, AI and analytics tools may store/process data outside India or in unknown locations |
| Long retention | Leads, inactive accounts, old recordings and test data may remain indefinitely |
| Dark-pattern risk | Forms may force phone/email for free content or pre-tick consent options |
| School/college partnerships | Role of EdTech as Data Fiduciary or Data Processor may be unclear |
| Breach exposure | User databases, test results, certificates, call recordings and documents may be attractive breach targets |

## 18. Source and research notes

This template was prepared using the TrustStack™ DPDP Compass framework and current public/regulatory research as of 07 September 2026. The following source categories informed the template:

1. Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 — processing, Data Fiduciary, Data Principal, Data Processor, consent, children’s data, Consent Manager and accountability concepts.

4. Press Information Bureau advisory on caution regarding EdTech companies — references to EdTech companies, parents/students, child-safety guidance and e-commerce/consumer-risk context.
5. Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020 and related official consumer-protection references — relevant for EdTech subscription, digital platform, grievance, consumer interface and dark-pattern/data collection review.
6. Information Technology (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Rules, 2021 — conditionally relevant where EdTech platforms provide user-generated content, communities, public posting, marketplace/community interactions or intermediary-like services.

The EdTech template should be periodically reviewed because AI, children’s data, digital education, consumer-protection and intermediary rules can change.

## 19. Implementation caution

The questions in this file are intentionally detailed. In the live platform, they should be used dynamically, not all asked blindly to every EdTech organisation.

Recommended approach:

```text
Core DPDP questions
+ EdTech mandatory questions
+ children’s data pack if minors/age unknown
+ AI pack if AI/chabot/adaptive/proctoring exists
+ video pack if live classes/recordings exist
+ marketing pack if lead generation/campaigns exist
+ vendor/cloud pack if SaaS/cloud/vendors exist
+ B2B integration pack if schools/colleges/corporates upload data
```

This avoids assessment fatigue while preserving DPDP depth and industry relevance.
